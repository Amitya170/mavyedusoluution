High-Agreement Human Preference Curation for Multilingual LLM Alignment

Establishing Fleiss’ kappa and Cohen’s kappa > 0.85 in human feedback rater networks across 40+ languages to avoid reward model collapse.

Reward models trained on low-agreement or noisy human preference data inevitably collapse into sycophancy or hallucination. When evaluating frontier LLMs across low-resource or non-English prompts, rater alignment is the primary determinant of model safety and reasoning quality.

Mathematical Multi-Rater Consensus: Fleiss' Kappa

Rather than relying on simple percentage agreement, high-performance rater pools establish formal inter-annotator agreement metrics that discount chance agreement:

κ = (P̄ - P̄_e) / (1 - P̄_e)

// Quality Tier Thresholds:
κ < 0.40 : Discard batch (High noise / Ambiguous rubric)
0.60 ≤ κ < 0.80 : Substantial agreement
κ ≥ 0.85 : Gold-standard certified preference data (Production ready)

Adversarial Red-Teaming in Local Idioms

Standard safety filters built in English often fail to recognize regional slang, cultural insults, and obfuscated jailbreak attempts in Arabic, Hindi, or Tagalog. Native specialist red-teamers stress-test model safeguards against cultural edge cases before weights are deployed.
